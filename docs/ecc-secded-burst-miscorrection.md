# Why SECDED Silently Miscorrects Odd-Sized Burst Errors

This note explains the mechanism behind the burst-error results in
[rowhammer-demo-results-analysis.md](rowhammer-demo-results-analysis.md#on-die-ecc-capability-error-injection-model),
generated by `script/ecc_capability_test.py`. It answers a specific question:
why do contiguous 3-bit and 5-bit flips inside one ECC codeword always end in
**silent data corruption**, while 2-bit and 6-bit flips never do, and 4/8/12/16-bit
flips split roughly 50/50?

The short answer: it is an **ECC capability boundary**, triggered by **too many
simultaneous bit flips (>=3)**, and the exact failure mode (miscorrection vs.
total silence vs. safe detection) is then decided by whether the flip count is
odd or even plus incidental binary alignment — not by a simple "more bits is
worse" gradient.

## The two signals the decoder has

The model is a generic SECDED (single-error-correct, double-error-detect)
Hamming code. For a codeword with error positions `e1, e2, ..., em`, the
decoder only has two signals:

1. `total_parity` — the parity of *how many* bits flipped across the whole
   codeword (including the extra overall-parity bit). This is just `m mod 2`:
   odd or even. It never reveals the actual count.
2. `syndrome S` — `e1 XOR e2 XOR ... XOR em` (bitwise XOR of the error
   positions, computed over the classic Hamming bit range only). For exactly
   one error, `S` equals that error's position exactly; this identity is the
   entire trick behind Hamming codes.

Decoding is only four branches:

| `S` | `total_parity` | Decoder action | Correct only when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | reports "no error" | m = 0 |
| !=0 | 1 | flips bit `S`, reports "corrected" | m = 1, and `S` is that one true error position |
| !=0 | 0 | reports "uncorrectable" (safe) | any m >= 2, as long as this branch is reached |
| 0 | 1 | flips the overall-parity bit, reports "corrected" | practically never, in real data |

SECDED's contract is: **guaranteed correct for exactly 1 error, guaranteed
detected for exactly 2. For m >= 3, nothing is guaranteed** — the outcome falls
out of whichever branch the `(S, total_parity)` pair happens to land in.

## Why odd bursts (3, 5) are *always* miscorrected

`total_parity = m mod 2`. For any burst of size 3 or 5, `total_parity = 1`
**unconditionally**, regardless of which bits were hit. That forces the
decoder into the "flip bit `S`, report corrected" branch every single time —
it can never reach the safe "uncorrectable" branch, which requires
`total_parity = 0`.

So the decoder always commits to a single-bit fix, and one bit flip can never
repair 3 or 5 simultaneously-flipped bits — even in the best case.

**Worked example, burst = {10, 11, 12} (k=64 codeword, 72 bits total):**

```text
S = 10 ^ 11 ^ 12 = 01010 ^ 01011 ^ 01100 = 01101 = 13
```

`total_parity = 1` (3 flips) -> decoder flips bit 13, which was never wrong to
begin with. Result: bits 10, 11, 12 are still corrupted, and bit 13 is now
corrupted too. Reported: "corrected." Actual: 4 wrong bits.

**Worked example, burst = {10, 11, 12, 13, 14} (width 5):**

```text
S = 10 ^ 11 ^ 12 ^ 13 ^ 14 = 14
```

Here `S` happens to coincide with one of the real error positions (14). The
decoder flips it back, genuinely repairing one of the five. But bits
10, 11, 12, 13 are still wrong, so the data still does not match the original.

That is the generalization: **whether `S` coincides with a true error
position is irrelevant.** You cannot repair 3+ simultaneously-flipped bits by
flipping only 1 bit. Once `total_parity` forces the "attempt a single-bit fix"
branch, failure for m >= 3 is mathematically certain, not probabilistic. That
is why widths 3 and 5 show exactly 100.0000% SDC with zero variance across
repeats in `ramulator_out/ecc_capability/summary.md` — there is no randomness
left to average over.

## Why even bursts (2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16) split differently

For even m, `total_parity = 0` always, so the decoder never attempts a blind
fix. It only ever chooses between "safely flag uncorrectable" (`S != 0`) or
"falsely claim totally clean" (`S == 0`) — a coincidence of binary alignment,
unrelated to the error count.

**Width 2, burst {10, 11}:**

```text
S = 10 ^ 11 = 1
```

Always nonzero, because two distinct positions can never XOR to 0. Width 2 is
always safely detected.

**Width 4, burst {8, 9, 10, 11}:**

```text
S = 8 ^ 9 ^ 10 ^ 11 = 01000 ^ 01001 ^ 01010 ^ 01011 = 00000 = 0
```

`S = 0` and `total_parity = 0` -> the decoder reports "no error." This is the
worst outcome: worse than a false "corrected," because nothing is flagged.

**Width 4, burst {1, 2, 3, 4}:**

```text
S = 1 ^ 2 ^ 3 ^ 4 = 001 ^ 010 ^ 011 ^ 100 = 100 = 4
```

`S != 0` -> safely detected.

So for width 4 (and 8, 12, 16), roughly half of all starting alignments give
`S = 0` (silent, undetected) and half give `S != 0` (safely caught) — hence
the observed ~49%/51% split, stable across repeats because it depends on
which half of the codeword's bit positions the burst lands on, not on
statistical noise.

**Width 6** never produced an `S = 0` alignment anywhere in the 72-bit (k=64)
or 137-bit (k=128) codeword across every sampled start in this test — that is
why it is 100% safely detected. This is a property of these specific codeword
lengths' bit numbering, not a general guarantee that 6-bit bursts are always
safe.

## Summary table

| Burst width | Flip-count parity | Mechanism | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | odd | designed case: `S` is the one true error | 100% correctly fixed |
| 2 | even | `S` always != 0 for 2 distinct positions | 100% safely detected |
| 3, 5 | odd | `total_parity` forces a blind single-bit "fix"; one flip can't repair >= 3 flips | **100% silent miscorrection** |
| 4, 8, 12, 16 | even | coin-flip on whether the burst's XOR cancels to 0 | ~50% falsely "clean" (undetected), ~50% safely detected |
| 6 | even | XOR never canceled in this codeword's tested range | 100% safely detected |

## Bottom line

This is not "too many bits inverted" in a naive linear sense: width 6 has
more flipped bits than width 5, yet is completely safe, and width 4 has more
flipped bits than width 3, yet is only half as bad. It is an **ECC capability
boundary** — SECDED is only rated for <=2 simultaneous errors — combined with
the **parity of the error count** (odd vs. even), which decides whether the
decoder blindly attempts (and always fails) a "correction," or instead lands
on a coin-flip between safe detection and complete silence, governed by the
incidental binary alignment of the exact bit positions hit.

This directly matters for RowHammer: a real hammering attack does not always
produce a single clean victim-row bit flip. If enough weak cells in the same
row/codeword neighborhood flip together, the failure mode is not "ECC catches
it" — depending on how many cells flip and their exact position alignment, it
can be a guaranteed, silent miscorrection of the kind quantified here.
